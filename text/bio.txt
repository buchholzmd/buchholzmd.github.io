<p>
I'm a PhD candidate in the department of Computer Science at the University of British Columbia, co-advised by
<a href=https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~schmidtm/>Mark Schmidt</a>,
<a href=https://djsutherland.ml/>Danica Sutherland</a>,
and <a href=https://geoffpleiss.com/>Geoff Pleiss</a>.
My research focuses on applying theory to predict and improve real training behavior in large-scale machine learning models. I am interested in
the interplay of optimization dynamics and feature learning in neural networks, and particularily how large learning rates in gradient-based
stochastic optimization methods improve training stability and generalization in large language and vision models.</p>

<p>Previously, I earned a Master's in Mathematics at the Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences at NYU, where I completed my thesis under
<a href=https://cims.nyu.edu/~gunturk/>Sinan Güntürk</a>
and <a href=https://cims.nyu.edu/~bruna/>Joan Bruna</a>.
Before that, I received my BS in Computer Science and BA in Physics with a minor in Mathematics from
California State University, Long Beach, where I worked with <a href=https://home.csulb.edu/~wzhang2/>Wenlu Zhang</a>.
I also spent two years as a Machine Learning Engineer at Samsung Research America with the Think Tank Team working under Rehmi Post, where I
implemented generative models for problems in computational imaging, inverse rendering, and
audio synthesis, fine-tuned large-scale time-series transformer models for activity recognition from video data, and applied kernel methods for
temperature prediction from raw audio.</p>
